# Hypothesis and study design

Turn observations into competing explanations and tests that could distinguish them.

## Entry conditions

Record the observation or problem, the available evidence, the target population or system, and whether the work is exploratory or confirmatory. If an evidence review exists, preserve its source, evidence, claim, gap, boundary, and cutoff identities. If no evidence review exists, label the hypotheses as exploratory and avoid novelty claims.

Read [references/study-card.md](references/study-card.md) when a study, experiment, preregistration, or statistical analysis plan is required.

Read the shared [research-lineage contract](../cost-efficient-orchestration/research-lineage.md) when hypotheses will be updated by data or handed to literature review, reproduction, project execution, writing, figures, or software. Do not copy the full evidence corpus into the hypothesis record.

## Work-unit model guidance

| Work unit | Execution contract | Capability lane |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extract observations, assumptions, constraints, and candidate confounders | Scout | Fast and economical |
| Turn frozen hypotheses into feasibility, measurement, and statistical-plan structure | Chief, direct | Balanced |
| Generate competing mechanisms and discriminating predictions, or judge causality and conflicting evidence | Chief, direct; Reviewer when independence is required | Strong reasoning |

Use fast execution for formatting or frozen fields, not for deciding the scientific story. Use the general orchestration route for work not listed here.

## Workflow

1. Separate observations, prior evidence, assumptions, interpretations, and bounded gaps. Preserve their incoming IDs when present.
2. Generate multiple plausible hypotheses, including a null or measurement explanation when relevant; link each candidate to the claims, gaps, and observations that motivated it.
3. For each hypothesis, state mechanism, scope, falsifiable predictions, and observations that would weaken it. Give hypotheses and predictions stable IDs and versions.
4. Identify shared predictions that cannot discriminate among hypotheses.
5. Propose the smallest feasible measurement or intervention that produces different expected outcomes across competitors.
6. Surface confounders, selection effects, leakage, reverse causation, measurement error, and plausible alternative explanations.
7. Define the estimand, unit of analysis, outcomes, uncertainty, missing-data handling, multiplicity, and sensitivity analyses before examining confirmatory results.
8. Mark the human freeze point for hypotheses, exclusions, outcomes, and analysis choices.
9. Emit bounded next actions: a missing-evidence query, a claim or method reproduction target, or a frozen study and implementation contract. Let Chief route each action separately.

## Required return

- observation and evidence status;
- lineage boundary and the claim, gap, and observation IDs used;
- competing hypothesis table;
- discriminating predictions and alternative explanations;
- proposed study with feasibility and ethics constraints;
- statistical plan at the level justified by available information;
- results that would support, weaken, or leave each hypothesis unresolved;
- stable hypothesis, prediction, and study IDs, plus bounded next-action handoffs when downstream work is required;
- decisions requiring domain-expert or human acceptance.

## Boundaries

- A hypothesis is not supported because it is coherent, novel-sounding, or generated by a strong model.
- Do not convert post hoc explanation into a preregistered prediction.
- If preliminary data generated or refined a hypothesis, testing it on the same data remains exploratory; confirmation requires untouched data, a new sample, or independent replication.
- New evidence creates a new version or status update; it does not silently rewrite a frozen hypothesis or study.
- Separate association, prediction, intervention, mediation, and causal identification.
- Do not optimize the design around a desired significant result.
- Ethics approval, clinical interpretation, primary claims, and irreversible study choices remain human decisions.
- These rows are starting points. Chief retains the final model, delegation, and S0–S4 decisions.

## Upstream adoption

- **Selected:** multiple competing hypotheses, a null or artifact explanation, falsifiable and discriminating predictions, confounder checks, an explicit estimand and statistical plan, stable evidence-to-hypothesis links, versioned updates, bounded downstream handoffs, and a human freeze point.
- **Not selected now:** autonomous hypothesis-ranking loops, model coherence or graph structure as evidence, a required persistent agent workspace, domain-specific laboratory automation, or irreversible study and ethics decisions by an agent.
